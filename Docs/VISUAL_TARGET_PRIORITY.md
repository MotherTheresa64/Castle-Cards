# Castle Cards — Approved Visual Target Priority

The four approved concept renders are ranked in this order and should be treated as the art-direction hierarchy for the medieval tavern vertical slice.

## 1 — Primary target

Use this as the composition lock.

- Player-seat viewpoint across a physical wooden table.
- Large but non-obstructive near castle in the lower foreground.
- Enemy castle at the far edge below the opponent.
- Brunette, bearded opponent clearly visible from the torso up.
- Rich low-poly diorama terrain with river, bridge, trees, rocks, paths and readable battle lanes.
- Warm lantern/candle key lighting with cool shadow fill.
- A few physical cards and reserve figurines visible at the player edge.
- The board should dominate the frame; HUD and room dressing are secondary.

## 2 — Secondary target

Use this primarily for the cheating/reserve area and physical-game feeling.

- Reserve miniatures physically lined up in front of the player.
- Dedicated tray/rack/token space that can support hiding, miscounting or manipulating pieces.
- Visible cards and deck without turning the foreground into a flat UI bar.
- Stronger terrain sculpting and tactical elevation.
- Opponent leaning toward the board rather than sitting like a distant statue.

## 3 — Tertiary target

Use this for interaction and asset detail.

- Higher castle/model detail.
- Opponent hand reaching toward the game state.
- Dense but readable troop/siege silhouettes.
- Strong authored low-poly forms rather than obvious primitives.

## 4 — Supplemental target

Use this mainly for tavern atmosphere.

- Rich shelves, banners, chains, barrels, lanterns and timber structure.
- Cinematic depth and environmental storytelling.
- Never allow room clutter to obstruct the opponent or battlefield.

## Non-negotiable quality rule

Do not solve visual shortcomings by merely increasing the number of props or changing the camera. The major visible assets — castles, opponent, battlefield, tabletop, hero miniatures and player reserve/card area — must be authored at the same stylized low-poly quality level as the approved concepts.

## Gameplay presentation rule

Castle Cards is a physical tabletop battle. Figurines in front of the player are intentional and support the cheating mechanic. Cards, reserve troops, casualties and hidden/false-count opportunities should be represented by physical objects on the table whenever practical rather than only through HUD counters.
